# 797 | A Next-Generation Narrowbody

**A business-driven exploration of a next-generation Boeing narrowbody aircraft.**

Concept Definition · Independent Study · CC0 1.0 Universal

## Executive Summary

The 797 Concept Study explores a potential clean-sheet, A321-class aircraft intended to address the upper narrowbody and 757 replacement markets.

The central thesis is that **airline economics, operational commonality and human factors should be fundamental aircraft design requirements rather than downstream considerations.**

The concept is built around three strategic pillars:

- **Pilot qualification efficiency:** Explore an approved differences-training pathway for existing 737 pilots.
- **Modern systems commonality:** <u> Prioritize </u> architectural and operational commonality with the 777 and 787 aircraft families.
- **Propulsion flexibility:** Evaluate compatibility with both open-fan propulsion and advanced conventional turbofan technology.

All proposed capabilities represent preliminary design objectives, not validated engineering or certification conclusions.

## Problem Statement

A clean-sheet aircraft presents an opportunity to improve operating economics, passenger experience and technological capability. However, introducing a new aircraft family can also impose significant costs and operational disruptions on airline operators.

These challenges include:

- Pilot qualification and recurrent training expenditures.
- Simulator, instructor and training infrastructure requirements.
- Reduced pilot availability during fleet transitions.
- Increased fleet and operational complexity.
- Uncertainty surrounding emerging propulsion technologies.

The study examines whether addressing these challenges during initial concept definition could improve the overall value proposition of a next-generation aircraft.

## Strategic Objectives

### 1. Pilot Qualification and Training

Explore the feasibility of maintaining sufficient commonality with the 737 family to support an approved differences-training pathway under an existing type rating.

Additionally, investigate potential Shortened Transition and Rating (STAR) pathways between the proposed 797 and the 777/787 families.

**These are certification-dependent objectives, not assumed outcomes.**

### 2. Flight Deck and Systems Architecture

Prioritize commonality with the 777 and 787 families where technically and operationally beneficial, including:

- Flight deck design philosophy.
- Avionics and aircraft systems architecture.
- Crew alerting and information presentation.
- Human-machine interfaces.
- Operating procedures and automation philosophy.
- Maintenance and diagnostic systems.

Commonality should deliver measurable operational value without unnecessarily inheriting legacy design limitations.

### 3. Propulsion Strategy

Evaluate an airframe architecture capable of supporting either:

- Next-generation open-fan propulsion.
- Advanced conventional turbofan propulsion.

The objective is to **reduce dependence** on a single propulsion technology while recognizing the potentially substantial engineering, certification and economic challenges associated with multiple engine configurations.

### 4. Airline Business Value

Evaluate the potential for:

- Reduced fleet introduction costs.
- Reduced pilot training expenditure and downtime.
- Improved cross-fleet operational flexibility.
- Simplified maintenance and training infrastructure.
- Competitive lifecycle operating economics.

Economic benefits must be evaluated against additional development, integration and certification costs.


## Design Principles

1. **Safety takes precedence over commonality.**
2. Business and operational requirements should inform engineering decisions.
3. Commonality should be retained where it provides demonstrable value.
4. Legacy design constraints should not be inherited without justification.
5. Regulatory compliance must be considered from initial concept definition.
6. Engineering assumptions should remain open to challenge and revision.

## Open Questions

The project actively seeks critical examination of its underlying assumptions.

- Is the proposed 737 qualification pathway technically, operationally and regulatory realistic?
- Where might 737 commonality conflict with modern 777/787 architecture?
- Would reduced qualification requirements produce meaningful airline savings?
- Would propulsion flexibility justify its additional development and integration costs?
- What operational, commercial or regulatory considerations have been overlooked?
- Under what circumstances should the original design objectives be abandoned or revised?

**Identifying a fundamental flaw in the proposal is considered a valuable project outcome.**
